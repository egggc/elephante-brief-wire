SAME_OCCURRENCE (the same occurrence): the same actor doing the same thing to the same concrete object at the same time. Cross-language retellings, different outlets' emphasis, more or less detail, and the official text versus press coverage do not change identity. The details of one decision (its scope, exemptions, effective date, the full text or a fact sheet) are the same occurrence; so are different reports of the same ruling, filing, dataset, survey, incident, single interview or speech, and repeated promotion of the same campaign or deadline. The shared identity must land on a concrete decision or occurrence, not merely the same government, company, day, meeting or umbrella announcement: separate decisions, speeches or deals inside it are judged separately.

SAME_STORY (different developments of the same event): not the same occurrence, but in direct sequence around one concrete occurrence: a leak or rumour and the formal decision; a draft rule and the final rule; an announcement and its taking effect, enforcement or suspension; a decision and the other side's response, retaliation, appeal or investigation; a deal and its approval, rejection or closing; an event and the official response, further disclosure or follow-up statement. Continued investigation of one exposed act may add affected parties, but the material must support the follow-up relation. Two independent decisions, results or deals are not one event just because they share a country, company, sector or week.

UNRELATED (different things): different decisions, deals or reports (different dates or different actors); two different things the same government or company did on one day; different studies, opinions or incidents on the same theme; merely mentioning the same country, company or policy. Explainers, opinion and commentary about a policy are UNRELATED unless they clearly address that one concrete occurrence (analyse it, respond to it, report its effect).

ROUNDUP: one side gathers several independent occurrences and includes the other's event as one item among many: multi-topic briefings, daily or weekly digests, summit recaps, overviews of several deals or speakers. A roundup and a single-event report can be neither SAME_OCCURRENCE nor SAME_STORY, and a roundup mentioning two events does not merge them. Using other events only as background or comparison is judged by its core occurrence, not as a roundup.
